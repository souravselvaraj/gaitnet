## Q1. Which Improbable AI Lab papers (2024-2026) address legged/humanoid locomotion, loco-manipulation, reward design, exploration, sim-to-real, constrained RL and related topics? (title, authors, year, venue, link, core idea, key results)

### Takeaway
From 2024 to 2026 the lab put out few pure locomotion papers. Its legged and humanoid output moved toward **loco-manipulation and whole-body control**: force control, athletic throwing on a B2+Z1, and compliant humanoid motion tracking. It also produced **controller/action-space "learnability" studies** (Action Space Design, CoRL 2024; Tune to Learn, 2026) and co-authored a **model-based contact-planning paper** for a hexapod (2026). Its most transferable contributions for GaitNet are RL-method papers on **constrained reward shaping**: EIPO's legged application, ICRA 2024, and HEPO. Behaviour multiplicity and gait parameterisation (Walk These Ways) and agile locomotion (Rapid Locomotion, DribbleBot) are pre-2024 foundations.

### Cited Findings

**A. Legged / humanoid locomotion and whole-body control (2024-2026)**

- **Agile asymmetric multi-legged locomotion: contact planning via geometric mechanics and spin model duality.** J. Habala, G. B. Margolis, T. Wang, P. Bhatt, J. He, N. Naeem, Z. Xu, P. Agrawal, D. I. Goldman, D. Luo, B. Chong. arXiv 2602.09123, 9 Feb 2026. No venue is listed. The comments field carries an arXiv admin note about text overlap with arXiv:2302.03019. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.09123)
  - The work is led by Penn State (Chong, corresponding author), Georgia Tech Physics (Goldman) and Tsinghua Physics (Luo, corresponding author). Margolis and Agrawal are listed as MIT Improbable AI Lab. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.09123v1)
  - Core idea: geometric mechanics reduces contact-rich gait planning to a graph optimisation. Each node pairs a contact pattern with a body shape. Shape-change edges carry displacement weights, contact-switch edges carry zero weight, and each switch costs a penalty λ. The goal is the cycle that maximises displacement minus switching cost. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.09123v1)
  - Spin-model duality: contact patterns are Z_N (Potts-type) spin states on shape sites, with λ acting as nearest-neighbour coupling. The authors prove a ground-state equivalence to a long-range Ising model (Theorem 1), in which domain walls are contact switches. The search is a "domain-wall search": for a fixed number K of switches it enumerates the configurations and keeps the lowest-energy one, which the authors claim is polynomial for finite K. It is not annealing. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.09123v1)
  - The hexapod uses 24 allowed contact states: 22 with at least four legs in stance, plus the two alternating-tripod states. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.09123v1)
  - Hardware results (body length 26.5 cm), in body lengths (BL) per cycle:
    - physics-informed small-amplitude gait: 0.61 ± 0.01 BL/cycle;
    - physics-informed large-amplitude gait: 0.56 ± 0.03;
    - best bio-inspired tripod: 0.48 ± 0.01;
    - "extended quadrupedal" gait: 0.41 ± 0.01;
    - a PPO-trained gait (Isaac Lab, open-loop forward-velocity baseline): 0.38. The RL gait looked like quadrupedal trotting.
    - The optimal gait is asymmetric (fast clockwise and slow counter-clockwise body yaw), and two unactuated legs could be replaced with rigid parts with no measured loss.
    - Future work proposed: use the physics-informed gaits as priors for learning controllers.
    — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.09123v1)
- **Tune to Learn: How Controller Gains Shape Robot Policy Learning.** A. Bronars, Y. Park (equal contribution), P. Agrawal. arXiv 2604.02523, 2 Apr 2026, preprint. Project: younghyopark.me/tune-to-learn. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.02523)
  - Argument: choose low-level PD gains for "learnability" under the learning paradigm in use, not for the task's desired stiffness. Effective task stiffness comes mostly from the policy's reactions. In one test, a stiff-behaviour policy reached about 5x higher effective Cartesian stiffness than a compliant one despite 32x lower actuator stiffness. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.02523v1)
  - Setup: gain regimes are the corners of a Kp/Kd grid (compliant/stiff × over/under-damped). RL covered 7 tasks on Franka FR3, an Allegro hand and **Unitree G1 humanoid velocity tracking**, the only locomotion task. BC covered 6 FR3 manipulation tasks. Sim-to-real used FR3 reaching. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.02523v1)
  - Results:
    - RL from scratch reached 99%+ success in every gain regime over more than two orders of magnitude of gains, once hyperparameters and environment shaping were re-tuned. G1 in the compliant-underdamped regime had only one viable configuration.
    - BC did best with compliant-overdamped gains.
    - For sim-to-real, stiff-overdamped gains had more than 10x lower system-identification error but the largest zero-shot trajectory error, from high-frequency oscillation that domain randomisation did not remove. Lowering the policy rate cut jitter failures from 21.8% at 100 Hz to 5.0% at 10 Hz.
    — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.02523v1)
- **Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap for Athletic Loco-Manipulation.** N. Fey, G. B. Margolis, M. Peticco, P. Agrawal. arXiv 2502.10894, Feb 2025. The lab page lists it as RSS 2025; the arXiv page lists no venue. Project: uan.csail.mit.edu. — [lab page](https://people.csail.mit.edu/pulkitag/); [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.10894)
  - Two-stage pipeline:
    - An **Unsupervised Actuator Net (UAN)**, trained on real data without torque sensing, closes the actuator sim-to-real gap.
    - A whole-body controller is pre-trained, then fine-tuned with task rewards using reference trajectories as initial "hints" for exploration.
    — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.10894)
  - Robot: Unitree B2 quadruped with a modified Z1 Pro arm (19 actuated joints, 60 kg).
  - UAN details:
    - Real data: about 50 s of square- and sine-wave data per actuator plus about 55 min of Gaussian-noise data.
    - Training: RL on the joint-position discrepancy between sim and real transitions, so no torque labels are needed.
    - Architecture: a 2×128 MLP over 100 ms of position and velocity error history that outputs a corrective torque added at each 5 ms sim step.
    — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.10894v1)
  - Results:
    - Hardware: about 20 m ball throw, 5 and 10 lb dumbbell lifts held for more than 5 s, and a sled with a 113 N friction load pulled over 10 m.
    - UAN gave the smallest sim-to-real throw-distance gap. A supervised Actuator Net and CEM system ID worked in sim but transferred poorly.
    - Fine-tuning from the pre-trained WBC threw farther, with lower peak leg power, than training from scratch or a frozen WBC plus a high-level policy.
    - Task rewards were described as prone to reward hacking. The policy "leaned and fell" until environment changes were made.
    — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.10894v1)
- **SoftMimic: Learning Compliant Whole-body Control from Examples.** G. B. Margolis*, M. Wang*, N. Fey, P. Agrawal. arXiv 2510.17792, Oct 2025, preprint. Project: gmargo11.github.io/softmimic. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.17792)
  - Core idea: RL motion-imitation produces stiff controllers that rigidly reject deviations. SoftMimic instead uses an offline IK solver to build an augmented dataset of feasible compliant responses, and rewards the policy for matching those rather than the raw reference. The policy sees only the original reference, so it must infer external forces implicitly. Stiffness can be set at deployment with a joystick. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.17792); [project page](https://gmargo11.github.io/softmimic/)
  - The project page reports only qualitative comparisons (a stiff baseline damages a delicate structure or spills when pouring) and does not name the humanoid platform. — [project page](https://gmargo11.github.io/softmimic/)
- **Learning Force Control for Legged Manipulation.** T. Portela, G. B. Margolis, Y. Ji, P. Agrawal. ICRA 2024. arXiv 2405.01402. — [lab page](https://people.csail.mit.edu/pulkitag/); [arXiv search listing](https://arxiv.org/search/?query=Agrawal%2C+Pulkit&searchtype=author&abstracts=show&order=-announced_date_first&size=100)
  - RL policies for direct force control on a quadruped with an arm, without force sensors, giving gravity compensation and impedance control. An operator commands only the manipulator and the body adjusts automatically. The authors claim it as the first deployment of learned whole-body force control on legged manipulators. — [arXiv search listing (abstract)](https://arxiv.org/search/?query=Agrawal%2C+Pulkit&searchtype=author&abstracts=show&order=-announced_date_first&size=100)
- **Maximizing Quadruped Velocity by Minimizing Energy.** S. Mahankali*, C.-C. Lee*, G. B. Margolis, Z.-W. Hong, P. Agrawal. ICRA 2024. — [project page](https://srinathm1359.github.io/eipo-locomotion/); [IEEE](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/abstract/document/10609983)
  - Applies Extrinsic-Intrinsic Policy Optimization (EIPO) to locomotion. Instead of hand-tuning the weight of an energy term, it poses a constrained problem: minimise energy only within the set of task-optimal policies. In simulation EIPO beat PPO on task performance at comparable energy, it beat PPO on the real robot, and it worked with a very simple auxiliary reward for omnidirectional velocity tracking. — [project page](https://srinathm1359.github.io/eipo-locomotion/)
- **Action Space Design in Reinforcement Learning for Robot Motor Skills.** J. Eßer, G. B. Margolis, O. Urbann, S. Kerner, P. Agrawal. CoRL 2024, PMLR v270, pp. 4021-4032. — [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v270/esser25a.html); [Lamarr Institute](https://lamarr-institute.org/publication/action-space-design-in-reinforcement-learning-for-robot-motor-skills/)
  - Compares joint position, velocity and torque action spaces on a wheeled-legged robot, a quadruped and a simulated locomotion/manipulation/control suite. The effect of action space is substantial and task-dependent. Much of it comes from **policy initialisation** and **inter-timestep behaviour**, meaning what the robot does between control steps. — [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v270/esser25a.html)
- **Rapid Locomotion via Reinforcement Learning.** G. Margolis, G. Yang, K. Paigwar, T. Chen, P. Agrawal. Mini Cheetah reaches up to 3.9 m/s on grass, ice and gravel, using an adaptive curriculum on velocity commands plus online system identification. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.02824)
  - Venue conflict: the lab page lists "IJRR 2024 (RSS 2022)" ([lab page](https://people.csail.mit.edu/pulkitag/)), while the arXiv comments field says only "Accepted ... RSS 2022" ([arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.02824)).

**B. RL-method papers (reward design, exploration, constraints, multimodality) relevant to locomotion**

- **Going Beyond Heuristics by Imposing Policy Improvement as a Constraint (HEPO).** C.-C. Lee, Z.-W. Hong, P. Agrawal. Code: github.com/Improbable-AI/hepo. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.05328)
  - Venue conflict: the lab page lists NeurIPS 2024 with OpenReview id vBGMbFgvsX ([lab page](https://people.csail.mit.edu/pulkitag/); [OpenReview PDF](https://openreview.net/pdf?id=vBGMbFgvsX)). arXiv v1 was posted 7 Jul 2025 with no venue field ([arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.05328)).
  - Formulation: maximise J(π)+H(π) subject to J(π) ≥ J(π_H), where J is task return, H is heuristic return and π_H is a heuristic-only policy. It is solved as a Lagrangian min-max: the policy is trained on (1+α)·r + h, with α updated from the task-return gap. Both policies are trained jointly with PPO and share trajectories, each collecting half the batch. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.05328v1)
  - Results:
    - Benchmarks: 29 tasks (9 IsaacGym, including Anymal, Ant and Humanoid, plus 20 Bi-DexHands), 5 seeds, IQM of normalised return. HEPO had the highest IQM and about 62% probability of improvement over the heuristic-only policy.
    - J+H with a fixed weight did slightly worse than heuristic-only, which the authors attribute to sensitivity to the weight λ.
    - With heuristics written by 12 non-expert graduate students for FrankaCabinet, HEPO scored IQM 0.94 against 0.44 for heuristic-only, and won on 9 of 12 rewards.
    - Using the heuristic-only policy as the constraint reference beat using the task-only policy, which is EIPO's choice.
    — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.05328v1)
- **ORSO: Accelerating Reward Design via Online Reward Selection and Policy Optimization.** C. B. C. Zhang, Z.-W. Hong, A. Pacchiano, P. Agrawal. ICLR 2025. arXiv 2410.13837. Treats the choice among candidate shaping rewards as online model selection with regret guarantees. It is up to 8x faster, finds rewards more than 50% better than prior methods, and on average matches expert-engineered rewards. — [lab page](https://people.csail.mit.edu/pulkitag/); [arXiv search listing (abstract)](https://arxiv.org/search/?query=Agrawal%2C+Pulkit&searchtype=author&abstracts=show&order=-announced_date_first&size=100)
- **Random Latent Exploration for Deep RL (RLE).** S. Mahankali, Z.-W. Hong, A. Sekhari, A. Rakhlin, P. Agrawal. ICML 2024. arXiv 2407.13755. Explores by pursuing randomly sampled goals in a latent space, with no action noise and no bonus bookkeeping. On average it beats noise- and bonus-based exploration on Atari and Isaac Gym; the abstract gives no figures. — [arXiv search listing (abstract)](https://arxiv.org/search/?query=Agrawal%2C+Pulkit&searchtype=author&abstracts=show&order=-announced_date_first&size=100); [lab page](https://people.csail.mit.edu/pulkitag/)
- **Learning Multimodal Behaviors from Scratch with Diffusion Policy Gradient (DDiffPG).** NeurIPS 2024. arXiv 2406.00681. The first author is listed as "Zechu Li" on arXiv and "Steven Li" on the lab page; co-authors are R. Krohn, T. Chen, A. Ajay, P. Agrawal and G. Chalvatzaki. An actor-critic method that trains diffusion policies from scratch and keeps multiple behaviour modes, using unsupervised clustering, novelty-based intrinsic motivation and mode-specific Q-learning. It shows online replanning in mazes with unseen obstacles; the abstract gives no numbers. — [arXiv search listing (abstract)](https://arxiv.org/search/?query=Agrawal%2C+Pulkit&searchtype=author&abstracts=show&order=-announced_date_first&size=100); [lab page](https://people.csail.mit.edu/pulkitag/)
- **Position: Automatic Environment Shaping is the Next Frontier in RL.** Y. Park, G. Margolis, P. Agrawal. ICML 2024 Position track (Oral). arXiv 2407.16186. Argues that RL research should automate environment design (observations, actions, rewards and simulation dynamics) rather than mainly tune algorithms. It contains no quantitative results. — [lab page](https://people.csail.mit.edu/pulkitag/); [arXiv search listing (abstract)](https://arxiv.org/search/?query=Agrawal%2C+Pulkit&searchtype=author&abstracts=show&order=-announced_date_first&size=100)
- **Vector Policy Optimization: Training for Diversity Improves Test-Time Search.** R. Bahlous-Boldi, ..., Z.-W. Hong, P. Agrawal. arXiv 2605.22817, May 2026. **LLM setting, not robotics.** Treats rewards as vectors and trains for a diverse solution set as a drop-in replacement for GRPO's advantage. It matches or beats scalar RL on pass@k and best@k, and the gap grows with search budget. — [arXiv search listing](https://arxiv.org/search/?query=Agrawal%2C+Pulkit&searchtype=author&abstracts=show&order=-announced_date_first&size=100)
- **From Imitation to Refinement: Residual RL for Precise (Visual) Assembly (ResiP).** L. Ankile, A. Simeonov, I. Shenfeld, M. Torne, P. Agrawal. ICRA 2025. arXiv 2407.16677. A closed-loop residual policy trained with RL on top of a frozen chunked BC policy. — [lab page](https://people.csail.mit.edu/pulkitag/); [arXiv search listing (abstract)](https://arxiv.org/search/?query=Agrawal%2C+Pulkit&searchtype=author&abstracts=show&order=-announced_date_first&size=100)
- **Reconciling Reality through Simulation (RialTo).** M. Torne, A. Simeonov, Z. Li, A. Chan, T. Chen, A. Gupta, P. Agrawal. RSS 2024. arXiv 2403.03949. Real-to-sim-to-real: RL fine-tuning in digital twins built from small real datasets, with "inverse distillation" of demonstrations into sim. Reported robustness gains of more than 67% on real tasks. — [arXiv search listing (abstract)](https://arxiv.org/search/?query=Agrawal%2C+Pulkit&searchtype=author&abstracts=show&order=-announced_date_first&size=100)
- **Diffusion Policy Policy Optimization (DPPO).** A. Z. Ren, J. Lidard, L. L. Ankile, A. Simeonov, P. Agrawal, A. Majumdar, B. Burchfiel, H. Dai, M. Simchowitz. ICLR 2025. arXiv 2409.00588. Best practices for fine-tuning diffusion policies with policy-gradient RL, with zero-shot sim-to-real on long-horizon manipulation. — [lab page](https://people.csail.mit.edu/pulkitag/); [arXiv search listing](https://arxiv.org/search/?query=Agrawal%2C+Pulkit&searchtype=author&abstracts=show&order=-announced_date_first&size=100)
- **RL's Razor: Why Online RL Forgets Less.** I. Shenfeld, J. Pari, P. Agrawal. arXiv 2509.04259, 2025. The lab page lists "ICLR 2025" plus a NeurIPS 2025 CCFM workshop best paper, a venue inconsistency with the Sep 2025 arXiv date. Forgetting tracks KL(fine-tuned ‖ base) on the new task, and on-policy RL implicitly prefers KL-minimal solutions. Validated on LLMs and robotic foundation models. — [lab page](https://people.csail.mit.edu/pulkitag/); [arXiv search listing](https://arxiv.org/search/?query=Agrawal%2C+Pulkit&searchtype=author&abstracts=show&order=-announced_date_first&size=100)

LfH relabels failed rollouts with the behaviour they actually achieved and matches GRPO with about 5x fewer rollouts on LIBERO-PRO. On a real Franka it reaches 56% success after 160 rollouts against 22% for GRPO. — [arXiv search listing](https://arxiv.org/search/?query=Agrawal%2C+Pulkit&searchtype=author&abstracts=show&order=-announced_date_first&size=100)

**D. [FOUNDATIONAL, pre-2024] legged work from the lab**

- **Walk These Ways: Tuning Robot Control for Generalization with Multiplicity of Behavior.** G. B. Margolis, P. Agrawal. CoRL 2022 (oral). arXiv 2212.03238. — [project page](https://gmargo11.github.io/walk-these-ways/)
  - Multiplicity of Behaviour (MoB): one policy encodes a structured family of strategies that solve the training tasks in different ways. The strategy is chosen at run time for out-of-distribution tasks without retraining. — [project page](https://gmargo11.github.io/walk-these-ways/)
  - Command = 3-D velocity (vx, vy, yaw rate) plus an **8-D behaviour vector**: three foot-timing (phase) offsets θ1-θ3 that set the gait, stepping frequency, body height, body pitch, stance width and footswing height. — [ar5iv full text](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2212.03238)
  - Gaits are imposed through "augmented" auxiliary rewards: swing-phase foot-force penalties and stance-phase foot-velocity penalties that follow the commanded contact schedule, footswing-height tracking, and a Raibert-heuristic foot-placement term. "Fixed" auxiliary rewards cover stability and smoothness. The **total reward is the task reward multiplied by exp(auxiliary terms)**, so progress is still rewarded when penalties are large. The controller runs on a Unitree Go1 at 50 Hz in both training and deployment (Isaac Gym, PPO). — [ar5iv full text](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2212.03238)
  - Generalisation: a higher footswing climbs stairs and curbs, a lower body height crawls under a 22 cm bar, a wider stance resists shoves, and adjusted frequency and swing push through bushes. On simulated platform terrain, survival was trot 0.88, pace 0.87, **gait-free baseline 0.83**, pronk 0.82 and bound 0.82. On flat ground the gait-free baseline tracked velocity best (0.94 against 0.83-0.92). — [ar5iv full text](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2212.03238)
  - Stated limitations: **a human pilot must choose the behaviour parameters**, and the parameterisation costs some in-distribution performance. Automatic selection, by imitation of humans or hierarchical self-tuning at deployment, is proposed as future work but not implemented. — [ar5iv full text](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2212.03238)
- **DribbleBot: Dynamic Legged Manipulation in the Wild.** Y. Ji, G. B. Margolis, P. Agrawal. ICRA 2023. arXiv 2304.01159. — [lab page](https://people.csail.mit.edu/pulkitag/)
- **Learning to See Physical Properties with Active Sensing Motor Policies.** G. B. Margolis, X. Fu, Y. Ji, P. Agrawal. CoRL 2023, PMLR v229. arXiv 2311.01405. Labels terrain physics (for example friction) from the robot's own locomotion. "Active sensing motor policies" learn behaviours, such as swiping a foot, that make the parameters easier to estimate. A vision model then predicts the properties from images and transfers to drone imagery. — [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v229/margolis23a.html); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.01405v1)
- **Visual Dexterity** (T. Chen et al., Science Robotics 2023, arXiv 2211.11744). In-hand reorientation of novel objects. — [lab page](https://people.csail.mit.edu/pulkitag/)
- EIPO and TGRL are the earlier constrained-RL foundations behind HEPO and the ICRA 2024 locomotion paper. EIPO (Chen et al., NeurIPS 2022) is cited inside HEPO as the constrained-objective predecessor. — [arXiv HTML (HEPO)](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.05328v1)

### Inferences
- The lab's locomotion line moved from "one policy, many gaits" (WTW 2022) and speed (Rapid Locomotion) to **whole-body / loco-manipulation and controller learnability** (UAN, SoftMimic, Force Control, Action Space Design, Tune to Learn). From 2024 to 2026 the lab published nothing that directly competes with learned **footstep or contact planning** on quadrupeds. The nearest is the model-based hexapod contact-planning paper, which Margolis and Agrawal co-authored. That leaves GaitNet's niche, a learned discrete contact or footstep selector with emergent gaits, open with respect to this lab.
- Gabriel Margolis is the lab's legged thread: he appears on WTW, Rapid Locomotion, DribbleBot, Active Sensing, Force Control, EIPO-locomotion, Action Space Design, Env Shaping, UAN, SoftMimic, DEXOP and the hexapod paper. A related-work section positioned against "Margolis/Agrawal" covers most of the lab's legged output.

## Q2. Which of the lab's ideas could address GaitNet's weaknesses (no-op preference, contact-rule enforcement, behaviour multiplicity and gait conditioning, controller/dynamics mismatch, candidate-generator dependence, slow perception, losing to a fixed trot)?

### Takeaway
The best levers are:
- **HEPO/EIPO-style constrained shaping**, to make "stepping" heuristics and energy or smoothness terms subordinate to task progress without hand-tuned weights, against the 85-95% no-op preference;
- **WTW-style behaviour conditioning** (a gait or stepping-cadence vector selectable at deployment), together with WTW's multiplicative task × exp(aux) reward;
- **Tune to Learn, UAN and Action Space Design**, for the convex-MPC to OCS2 controller gap;
- **evidence from WTW and the hexapod paper that structured gait priors often beat gait-free policies on robustness or speed**. This suggests repositioning GaitNet as learned contact-sequence deviation from, or selection among, gait priors rather than a pure gait-free planner.

### Cited Findings
- **Constrained shaping instead of weighted sums:**
  - HEPO maximises J+H subject to J(π) ≥ J(π_H) via a Lagrangian on (1+α)·r + h. With a fixed weight, J+H underperformed the heuristic-only policy because of weight sensitivity, while HEPO stayed robust to badly scaled non-expert heuristics (IQM 0.94 against 0.44). — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.05328v1)
  - EIPO for locomotion minimises energy only within task-optimal policies. It removed manual weight tuning and beat PPO on task performance in sim and on hardware. — [project page](https://srinathm1359.github.io/eipo-locomotion/)
- **Multiplicative reward form:** WTW multiplies the task reward by exp(auxiliary terms), so the agent is still rewarded for progress when penalties are high. — [ar5iv full text](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2212.03238)
- **Initialisation and inter-step behaviour:** much of the action-space effect on RL performance comes from policy initialisation and from what happens between control timesteps. — [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v270/esser25a.html)
- **Exploration:**
  - RLE explores by chasing random latent goals and beats noise- and bonus-based exploration on average in Atari and Isaac Gym. — [arXiv search listing](https://arxiv.org/search/?query=Agrawal%2C+Pulkit&searchtype=author&abstracts=show&order=-announced_date_first&size=100)
  - DDiffPG keeps multiple behaviour modes through clustering, novelty and mode-specific Q-functions. — [arXiv search listing](https://arxiv.org/search/?query=Agrawal%2C+Pulkit&searchtype=author&abstracts=show&order=-announced_date_first&size=100)
- **Curriculum:** Rapid Locomotion used an adaptive curriculum on velocity commands to reach 3.9 m/s. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.02824)
- **Behaviour multiplicity:** WTW conditions one policy on an 8-D gait and posture vector, and the right setting for a new environment is picked at run time. Its stated limitation is that a human picks it, and hierarchical self-tuning is left as future work. — [ar5iv full text](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2212.03238); [project page](https://gmargo11.github.io/walk-these-ways/)
- **Structured gaits vs gait-free:**
  - In WTW, trotting (0.88) and pacing (0.87) survived simulated platform terrain more often than the gait-free policy (0.83), and trot and pace used less power than gait-free at all tested speeds. The gait-free policy tracked velocity best on flat ground. — [ar5iv full text](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2212.03238)
  - In the hexapod paper, the optimised physics-informed contact sequence (0.61 BL/cycle) beat an Isaac Lab PPO gait (0.38 BL/cycle), and the PPO gait converged to a trot-like pattern. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.09123v1)
- **Controller gains and rate:** gains should be chosen for learnability. Stiff, overdamped gains minimise sys-ID error yet maximise the zero-shot sim-to-real trajectory error, and lowering the policy rate from 100 Hz to 10 Hz cut jitter failures from 21.8% to 5.0%. Re-tuning hyperparameters and environment shaping made every gain regime trainable with RL. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.02523v1)
- **Learned actuator correction from little real data:** UAN learns a corrective torque from about 50 s of wave data per actuator plus about 55 min of noise data, without torque sensing, by RL on sim/real transition discrepancy. A supervised Actuator Net and CEM system ID transferred worse. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.10894v1)
- **Residual correction over a frozen base policy:** ResiP adds a residual RL policy on a frozen BC policy for closed-loop correction ([arXiv search listing](https://arxiv.org/search/?query=Agrawal%2C+Pulkit&searchtype=author&abstracts=show&order=-announced_date_first&size=100)). RialTo fine-tunes in digital twins built from small real datasets, with robustness gains of more than 67% ([same](https://arxiv.org/search/?query=Agrawal%2C+Pulkit&searchtype=author&abstracts=show&order=-announced_date_first&size=100)).
- **Fine-tuning without forgetting:** RL's Razor shows that on-policy RL fine-tuning forgets less than SFT, with forgetting tracking the KL to the base policy. — [arXiv search listing](https://arxiv.org/search/?query=Agrawal%2C+Pulkit&searchtype=author&abstracts=show&order=-announced_date_first&size=100)
- **Automating environment design:**
  - The Automatic Environment Shaping position paper argues that observation, action, reward and dynamics design should be automated rather than hand-tuned. — [arXiv search listing](https://arxiv.org/search/?query=Agrawal%2C+Pulkit&searchtype=author&abstracts=show&order=-announced_date_first&size=100)
  - ORSO selects among candidate shaping rewards online with regret guarantees, at up to 8x speed-up. — [same](https://arxiv.org/search/?query=Agrawal%2C+Pulkit&searchtype=author&abstracts=show&order=-announced_date_first&size=100)
- **Terrain properties from locomotion:** Active Sensing Motor Policies (2023) label terrain properties from the robot's own locomotion and learn image-to-property predictors. — [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v229/margolis23a.html)

### Inferences
- **No-op preference (85-95% no-ops) as a shaping-weight pathology.** If GaitNet's reward combines progress with stability, energy or swing-cost terms through fixed weights, a "never step unless forced" policy is the classic failure that EIPO and HEPO were built for. Three concrete options:
  - **HEPO:** add a stepping or progress heuristic h (for example a Raibert-style "feet keep up with body" term, or a bonus for a swing that lands on a valid foothold), with the task return J (traversal progress or success) constrained not to fall below the h-only policy.
  - **EIPO:** recast the stability or energy terms as the secondary objective, optimised only within task-optimal policies.
  - **WTW:** use the task × exp(aux) multiplicative form so penalties cannot zero out progress reward.
  These are cheap to try because the lab's HEPO code is public (github.com/Improbable-AI/hepo).
- **Initialisation and exploration of the no-op logit.** Action Space Design shows that initialisation drives much of the action-space effect. For an argmax over 16×4 candidates plus a no-op, the initial no-op logit and score scale likely shape the early-training basin. Options: bias initialisation away from the no-op, use RLE-style latent-goal exploration, or apply a Rapid-Locomotion-style velocity curriculum that forces stepping early. All are plausible, cheap ablations.
- **Behaviour multiplicity and gait conditioning.** Condition GaitNet's scoring network on a WTW-style behaviour vector: desired step cadence, maximum concurrent swings (1 or 2), swing-ordering preference (diagonal pairs or a lateral sequence), footswing height, and a "risk" or step-length preference. This makes GaitNet a MoB footstep planner whose behaviour is chosen at run time, for example cautious single swings over voids and trot-like pairs on flat ground. It also reverses WTW's limitation: WTW needs a human to pick the gait; GaitNet is already an automatic contact-sequence selector. GaitNet could be framed as "the hierarchical self-tuning layer WTW calls for", choosing contacts directly rather than gait parameters.
- **Repositioning against "a fixed OCS2 trot beats GaitNet (45% vs ≤18%)".** The lab's own evidence says structured gaits often beat gait-free policies on robustness and efficiency: WTW's platform survival and power numbers, and the hexapod's optimised contact sequence against an RL gait. A defensible and more novel framing is **"learned deviation from a gait prior"**: GaitNet selects when to break or insert a swing relative to a nominal trot schedule (the no-op means "follow the prior"), or picks among a small library of prior gaits plus a free mode. That turns the trot baseline into GaitNet's floor rather than its competitor.
- **Sim-to-sim gap (convex MPC in training, OCS2 NMPC + WBC in deployment).**
  - Tune to Learn suggests evaluating the full policy-controller loop rather than how faithful the training controller is, and favouring lower policy rates or less stiff settings for transfer. GaitNet already runs at 25 Hz with discrete footstep outputs, which plausibly helps; this is an inference.
  - Three concrete options:
    - randomise the training controller (MPC horizon, gains, swing trajectory, timing) so the planner is controller-agnostic;
    - fit a UAN-style learned correction that makes convex-MPC rollouts reproduce OCS2 transitions, using a few minutes of Gazebo data;
    - fine-tune GaitNet in the deployment stack with a residual (ResiP) or with on-policy RL, which RL's Razor suggests limits forgetting.
- **Dependence on the candidate generator (ContactNet).** The Environment Shaping paper treats the action space as a design object to automate, and ORSO shows online selection among candidate shaping functions. By analogy, GaitNet could treat the candidate generator's parameters (sampling radius, count, ContactNet temperature) as shaping choices that are selected or learned, not fixed. This is an analogy; no lab paper does it for action sets.
- **Slow perception (3-4 Hz).** The lab's only relevant perception work is the 2023 Active Sensing line (self-supervised terrain-property labels from proprioception). That suggests a proprioceptive or contact-outcome-supervised foothold-validity predictor that could reduce reliance on the slow elevation-map pipeline. This is an inference; the lab has not applied it to footholds.

### Gaps
- None of the lab's 2024-2026 papers addresses **degenerate do-nothing policies in discrete-action locomotion** directly. The links above are by analogy from HEPO, EIPO and WTW.
- None addresses **sim-to-sim transfer between two model-based low-level controllers** (MPC to NMPC+WBC). Tune to Learn and UAN cover PD gains and actuators only.
- None addresses **fast terrain perception or elevation mapping** for footstep planning.
- The Tune to Learn G1 task is velocity tracking at the PD level. Whether its findings carry over to an MPC-mediated footstep interface is untested.

## Q3. Any lab work on discrete or hybrid action spaces, contact selection, or emergent gaits?

### Takeaway
Only one paper does **explicit discrete contact selection**: the 2026 hexapod paper with Margolis and Agrawal, which is model-based and not learned. It treats contact patterns as Potts or Ising spins with a switch penalty and finds optimal, asymmetric (emergent, non-bio-inspired) gaits by domain-wall search. The rest of the lab's legged RL uses continuous joint-level actions, either with prescribed contact schedules (WTW) or gait-free (Rapid Locomotion, the WTW baseline). No lab paper learns a discrete footstep or contact action space like GaitNet's, which supports GaitNet's novelty relative to this lab.

### Cited Findings
- **Discrete contact states and switching:** contact patterns are Z_N Potts spins on shape sites. Switching costs λ (nearest-neighbour coupling), the ground state is equivalent to a long-range Ising model (Theorem 1), and domain walls are contact switches. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.09123v1)
- **Feasible contact set and emergent gait:** the hexapod contact set is 24 states (≥4 legs in stance, plus two tripods). The optimal gait is asymmetric, with fast clockwise and slow counter-clockwise body yaw, and two same-side legs can stay in permanent contact. The authors present this as gait reorganisation through symmetry breaking. It ran at 0.61 BL/cycle, 50% faster than conventional gaits. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.09123v1); [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.09123)
- **RL comparison and future work:** an Isaac Lab PPO baseline converged to a trot-like quadrupedal pattern at 0.38 BL/cycle. Future work proposes physics-informed gaits as priors for learning controllers. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.09123v1)
- **Prescribed contact schedules:** WTW sets contact schedules (gaits) through three continuous phase offsets plus frequency, enforced by swing and stance auxiliary rewards. Gaits are commanded, not emergent. — [ar5iv full text](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2212.03238)
- **Action-space comparison:** Action Space Design compares only continuous joint-level spaces (position, velocity, torque). — [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v270/esser25a.html)
- **Discrete modes in other domains:** DDiffPG discovers and keeps discrete behaviour modes (clusters) with mode-specific Q-functions in continuous control. — [arXiv search listing](https://arxiv.org/search/?query=Agrawal%2C+Pulkit&searchtype=author&abstracts=show&order=-announced_date_first&size=100)
- **Diversity for search:** Vector Policy Optimization trains for diverse solution sets to aid test-time search, in LLMs. — [arXiv search listing](https://arxiv.org/search/?query=Agrawal%2C+Pulkit&searchtype=author&abstracts=show&order=-announced_date_first&size=100)

### Inferences
- **GaitNet as the learned counterpart.** The hexapod paper is the closest analogue: a combinatorial contact-sequence optimiser with a switching penalty and a feasible contact set, where GaitNet has at most two concurrent swings plus a no-op. GaitNet can be framed as the **learned, perception-conditioned, terrain-aware counterpart** of that model-based contact planning. That paper also gives a ready baseline and analysis tool: map GaitNet's per-tick decisions to a contact-state sequence and measure "domain walls", meaning switch counts per cycle and asymmetry. That quantifies emergent-gait structure, which was cited as missing.
- **A soft cost instead of the hard rule.** The switch penalty λ acting as a spin coupling suggests a soft alternative to GaitNet's hard "≤2 concurrent swings" mask: a learned or Lagrangian cost on switches and concurrency (the HEPO/EIPO machinery from Q2). Feasibility stays a hard mask, while the *preference* among feasible contact states becomes a tunable or constrained objective. This is an inference; no lab paper applies constrained RL to contact rules.
- **The niche is open.** Within this lab, gaits are either prescribed (WTW) or emerge implicitly from joint-level RL and collapse to trot-like patterns (hexapod PPO baseline, Rapid Locomotion). GaitNet's explicit, inspectable, discrete contact-decision layer above an MPC is not represented in the lab's 2024-2026 work. That supports novelty claims, provided GaitNet shows emergent non-trot contact sequences that matter on stepping-stone terrain.
- **Keeping several gait modes.** DDiffPG-style mode-specific critics, or VPO-style diversity objectives, could stop GaitNet's selection policy collapsing to a single conservative mode (always no-op), and keep a set of contact strategies alive for test-time selection. This is analogy only; neither paper is about locomotion.

### Gaps
- No 2024-2026 lab paper learns a discrete or hybrid (discrete contact plus continuous foothold) action space for legged robots, or emergent gaits from contact-level decisions. This was searched via the lab page and the full arXiv author listing.
- The hexapod paper does not handle terrain, perception or stepping stones, and it reports open-loop gait optimisation only. How it extends to quadrupeds on voids is unstated.
- No lab paper was found that applies Lagrangian or constrained RL to enforce contact or gait rules as constraints rather than masks.
