# Six labs leave learned foothold selection open

None of the six groups surveyed has published, in 2024-2026, a method in which a learned policy picks discrete footholds or contact events tick by tick. GaitNet's core idea is therefore unoccupied. The same literature also explains why: gaits, and footholds chosen implicitly, keep winning. In this corpus foothold choice comes in three forms. It is **implicit** inside end-to-end depth policies: a humanoid crossing 25 cm stones separated by 60 cm gaps ([RPL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03002)), and an A1 succeeding **94% on stepping stones** ([Agarwal et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2211.07638v1)). It is **prescribed** by a gait, as in Walk These Ways, Agile Continuous Jumping and Ye Zhao's gait-fixed MICP. Or it is **handed in** as a goal, and both WoCoCo and SceneBot name the missing high-level contact planner as future work ([WoCoCo](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.06005v2), [SceneBot](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.27581)). On robustness and speed, the comparisons in the notes favour structured gaits. In Walk These Ways a trot survives platform terrain at **0.88 vs 0.83** for gait-free, even though gait-free tracks velocity best on flat ground ([WTW](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2212.03238)). An optimized hexapod contact sequence covers **0.61 vs 0.38 body lengths per cycle** against a PPO gait ([hexapod](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.09123v1)). Our own result is 45% vs at most 18%. "Non-gaited beats gaited" cannot be defended on this evidence. What can be defended is narrower: learned discrete contact decisions as a gated, controller-agnostic layer that edits a gait where the gait fails, approximating gait-free contact planning at control rate. Reviewers will cite two lines of work first. One is Ye Zhao's quadruped foothold planners, which use the same terrain class and the same OCS2 stack (QuadPiPS scores **8/10 vs 3/10** for a heuristic-foothold baseline on sparse stones; [QuadPiPS](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.00112)). The other is the end-to-end depth line. The ranked directions are, in order, gated deviation from a gait prior; amortized gait-free contact planning with an asynchronous feasibility certificate; a controller-agnostic contact interface; a learned foothold C-space at camera rate; and contact-transition critics. Before any of them, the no-op problem deserves a free arithmetic check, because this repository's design notes show that an argmax over individual candidates against a single no-op atom, which is GaitNet's original selection rule, biases the choice toward waiting as the candidate count grows.

## Foothold choice is implicit, prescribed or handed in, never learned per tick

Across the six groups, a learned or optimized layer over a model-based or learned executor is the norm. A learned discrete contact decision is absent.

**LeCAR** usually keeps a model-based layer under a learned or sampled decision layer. Its quadruped walking still prescribes the gait: DIAL-MPC uses a trot foot-height generator, and Agile Continuous Jumping uses a fixed bounding gait whose frequency is learned ([DIAL-MPC](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.15610v1), [Agile Jumping](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.10923v2)).

**The Abbeel-affiliated Amazon FAR cluster** has moved almost entirely to humanoids distilled from privileged height maps into depth students, and never plans contacts explicitly ([RPL](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.03002), [PHP](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.15827)).

**Karen Liu's group** treats contact as a *command*. SceneBot raises terrain success from 60% to 100% by conditioning a tracker on binary per-link contact labels, but leaves "an autonomous high-level controller" for those labels to future work ([SceneBot](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.27581)).

**Ye Zhao's LIDAR Lab** owns the closest model-based comparators, but its planners fix contact order online. Gait-free MICP averages **27.23 s per solve** and runs only offline or as a repair step ([reactive synthesis](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.23185)).

**Agrawal's lab** co-authored the only explicit discrete contact optimizer in the survey, a model-based Potts/Ising formulation for a hexapod ([hexapod](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.09123v1)). Its legged RL otherwise uses continuous joint actions with commanded gaits.

**Pathak's lab** explicitly rejects elevation mapping plus foothold planning as "fragile" in favour of end-to-end depth control ([Agarwal et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.07638)).

The only *learned* discrete contact selector in the whole corpus is pre-2024 and non-cyclic. Liu's 2017 safe-falling policy picks the next contacting body part by taking the highest critic in a mixture of actor-critics ([safe falling](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.02905)).

The niche is open partly because the evidence in these notes favours gaits, prescribed or optimized. Walk These Ways' gait-free policy tracked velocity best on flat ground, but trot and pace survived rough platforms more often and used less power ([WTW](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2212.03238)). The hexapod's PPO baseline converged to a trot-like pattern and lost to an optimized contact sequence ([hexapod](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.09123v1)). On sparse rebar, Zhao's pure MIP planner rose from **60% to 90%** success when its horizon grew from 2 s to 3 s. It fell to 20-40% without a feasibility check ([reactive synthesis](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.23185)). Lookahead and certification separate success from failure there, and GaitNet's one-step argmax has neither.

GaitNet's novelty therefore has to come from *what* the learned layer decides: discrete, order-free contact events at 25 Hz, behind an inspectable, controller-agnostic interface. It cannot come from the hierarchy itself, and it cannot come from gait emergence in general, which Pathak's and Liu's labs showed years ago ([Fu et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.01674), [Yu, Turk, Liu](https://arxiv.org/abs/1801.08093)).

Papers by the cross-lab Amazon FAR collaboration appear in two or three of the six notes each. They are listed once, in their own table, so that one body of work is not counted as agreement among three labs.

## Every weakness has a donor mechanism, and two need a cheaper diagnosis first

The six notes map onto GaitNet's six weaknesses unevenly. Labelled for the rest of this report, they are **W1**, reluctance to step (85-95% no-ops); **W2**, the gap between the training controller and the deployment controller; **W3**, dependence on the candidate generator; **W4**, 3-4 Hz perception; **W5**, two concurrent swings helping only when the body is pulled along; and **W6**, the fixed trot winning on traversal. W2 and W6 have the most convergent evidence: every note offers a controller-gap mechanism, and five of the six independently propose a residual or gated layer over a gait. W1 and W4 should be diagnosed before they are researched.

### No-op reluctance may be partly arithmetic

The notes offer four competing explanations for W1. The Liu note reads it as mode collapse. The Agrawal note reads it as a shaping-weight pathology. The LeCAR note reads it as WoCoCo-style contact avoidance. A fourth explanation sits in this repository.

[ARCHITECTURE.md](../ARCHITECTURE.md#the-action-space-and-why-logits-get-corrected) documents two effects of candidate count. First, a flat softmax over candidates plus a single no-op atom makes stepping probability drift with K: **from about 0.12 at 16 candidates per leg to about 0.21 at 64**. Second, a deterministic argmax over individual candidate logits compares *one* foothold against the no-op. That biases the choice toward the no-op more strongly the more candidates are drawn. The original GaitNet's argmax over 16 candidates per leg plus a no-op is exactly this configuration.

The repo's evaluation already runs K=16/64/256 variants of the same policy ([paper.py](../packages/gaitnet-sim/src/gaitnet_sim/eval/paper.py)). If the no-op share moves with K, part of W1 is selection math, and reward surgery would be treating the wrong cause.

The 85-95% figure should also be restated as cadence. At 25 Hz with at most one lift-off per tick, it means 1.25-3.75 lift-offs per second: one four-leg cycle every 1.1-3.2 s. The repository's tuned scripted trot completes a cycle in 0.32 s ([paper.py](../packages/gaitnet-sim/src/gaitnet_sim/eval/paper.py)). Even that trot, launched one leg per tick, would leave half of all 25 Hz ticks as no-ops, so a high no-op share is not itself the pathology. The problem is a cadence 3-10x slower than the trot's and a step that lags the support margin, which is what the paper should measure.

### Perception at 3-4 Hz is probably an engineering limit before it is a research problem

The notes disagree on W4. The Pathak, Abbeel and LeCAR notes say to drop the map entirely and distill into a depth student, as RPL, Extreme Parkour and Agile Jumping do. The Zhao note points out that QuadPiPS's authors cap an elevation-mapping pipeline with plane decomposition at **25 Hz**, with the mapping step itself under 1 ms, and run superpixel segmentation at about 20 Hz on Go2 hardware ([QuadPiPS](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.00112)). The QuadPiPS extraction contains an unresolved timing conflict, so treat these rates as indicative.

Measure first. A learned C-space is a research contribution only if it beats a properly engineered fast map on edge violations. Locomotion Beyond Feet shows the opposite design choice is viable when perception stays slow: let the 3.1 Hz channel make only coarse, confidence-gated discrete decisions, while 50 Hz trackers carry the motion in between ([LBF](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.03607)).

### Our grid voids may be too forgiving for contact reasoning to pay off

The repository's fixed-trot baseline sends each foot to the valid foothold nearest its nominal spot ([variants.py](../packages/gaitnet-sim/src/gaitnet_sim/eval/variants.py)). On GaitNet's terrain it wins at 45%. Zhao's extended reactive-synthesis paper tested the same kind of nearest-feasible-foothold heuristic on sparse rebar at 0.48 m spacing, and it failed through foot trapping where the MICP planner completed the traversal ([reactive synthesis](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.23185)).

Two readings fit. Either the current void layouts are dense enough that "nearest valid cell on a trot schedule" is near-optimal, or GaitNet's policy is simply weak. A sparsity sweep separates the two readings. It is also the core experiment of the top-ranked direction below.

The controller gap, by contrast, is structural. SPI-Active-style parameter fitting cuts Go2 errors to 0.37-0.73 of vanilla ([SPI-Active](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.14266v1)), but ASAP reports that system-ID-style correction accumulates error over long horizons ([ASAP](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.01143v3)). Convex MPC and OCS2 NMPC differ in model and solver, not in parameters.

## Reviewers will cite Ye Zhao's planners and the end-to-end depth line first

The most dangerous prior work is the LIDAR Lab's quadruped contact planning. It runs on the same class of terrain (sparse stepping stones, rebar grids, balance beams), on Unitree Go2 hardware, and on the same OCS2 trajectory optimization, MPC and WBC stack that GaitNet deploys on.

QuadPiPS scores **8/10 on sparse stepping stones where a heuristic-foothold baseline on the same perception scores 3/10 and every elevation-map baseline scores 0/10** ([QuadPiPS](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.00112)). The reactive-synthesis line certifies every footstep with a convex MICP, and shows that dropping the feasibility check cuts pure-MIP success from 60-90% to 20-40% ([reactive synthesis](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.23185)).

GaitNet's honest answer is a side-by-side comparison:

| | Zhao lab planners | GaitNet |
|---|---|---|
| Contact order | Fixed online: a gait-fixed MICP, a "gait-based contact sequence" or a user-defined alternation | Order-free |
| Decision rate | Seconds per plan | 25 Hz |
| Feasibility | Certificate, or explicit kinematic checks per candidate | No guarantee |
| Horizon | Multi-step | One tick |

On the Zhao lab's own data, the guarantee and the lookahead are what decide sparse-terrain success.

The second line is end-to-end perceptive control. Agarwal et al. reached **94% on stepping stones** on a real A1 from one depth camera and argued that the map-plus-foothold-planning pipeline is fragile ([Agarwal et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2211.07638v1)). Extreme Parkour's public code makes an end-to-end baseline on GaitNet's terrain cheap to build. Reviewers who know this lab will expect one ([Extreme Parkour](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.14341)). RPL carries the argument to humanoids ([RPL](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.03002)).

The opening against this line is its failure mode. RPL's stepping-stone failures were edge landings. Extreme Parkour needed an explicit edge penalty, and still saw edge violations rise from 0.03 to 0.20 under a noisy map. A GaitNet paper that reports edge-violation rates beside an end-to-end baseline fights on ground of its own choosing.

## Five directions, ranked: lead with a gated layer over the trot

The ranking weighs novelty against the surveyed work, the number of weaknesses a direction addresses, and the cost of a first publishable result. The directions compose. Direction 2's certificate can serve as Direction 1's gate, and Direction 3 makes either one deployable.

| Rank | Direction | Weaknesses addressed | Closest prior work | Why it is novel, in one line | Decisive experiment |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Gated deviation-from-gait contact layer | W6, W1, W5 | WTW; Agile Jumping; RL-augmented MPC; ABS / Safe-Stop | First learned *discrete contact-event* edits (delay, advance, retarget, insert) on a gait prior, gated by a learned feasibility value | Success vs void-sparsity sweep: never below the trot, above it where nearest-valid trotting fails |
| 2 | Amortized gait-free contact planning with an asynchronous certificate | W3, W1, W6 | Zhao MICP / QuadPiPS; DIAL-MPC; safe falling | A control-rate approximation of an offline gait-free planner, with a Benders-style veto | Zhao's published layouts: success close to MICP at 25 Hz instead of seconds per plan |
| 3 | Controller-agnostic contact interface | W2 | ASAP; FADA; Workhorse; LocoFormer; SceneBot | First footstep policy shown to transfer across distinct low-level controllers | Train on convex MPC, test zero-shot on OCS2+WBC and a learned contact-prompted tracker |
| 4 | Camera-rate learned foothold C-space | W4, W3 | Agile Jumping; Agarwal 2022; RPL; QuadPiPS | Explicit discrete foothold choice over a learned, calibrated validity grid, aimed at end-to-end policies' edge failures | Perception-rate sweep, with edge violations vs an end-to-end depth baseline |

### 1. Make the trot GaitNet's floor, not its rival

**Idea.** The gait prior proposes its next contact events each tick. The prior is either the OCS2 trot or the repo's scripted trot that projects each foot onto the nearest valid foothold. GaitNet's discrete action set changes from "step somewhere or wait" to five kinds of edit: follow the prior (today's no-op), delay a scheduled lift-off, advance one, retarget the scheduled foothold to a sampled candidate, or insert an unscheduled step. A learned gate admits an override only when it is confident and otherwise lets the prior run. The gate can be an ABS/BAS-style reach-avoid value ([ABS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.17583), [BAS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.04276)) or Safe-Stop's rule of committing only when two estimators agree ([Safe Stop](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02358)). During training, PPF's model-assumption regularization can pull the policy toward the trot only in states where the trot's assumptions hold ([PPF](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.09833)).

**Why it is novel.** No surveyed paper edits individual contact events of a gait with a learned discrete policy. Walk These Ways needs a human to choose its gait vector and leaves automatic selection to future work ([WTW](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2212.03238)). Agile Continuous Jumping learns a stepping frequency and continuous Raibert residuals under a fixed bounding pattern ([Agile Jumping](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.10923v2)). Zhao's RL-augmented MPC learns gait frequency and swing shape but leaves footholds to Raibert, and its timing-only ablation reached **1%** on stairs ([RL-augmented MPC](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.18466)). The residual methods (RAMBO, ResMimic, residual off-policy RL) all add continuous corrections to continuous actions.

**Experiment.** Sweep void sparsity from the current grid layouts up to rebar-like spacing near the 0.48 m at which Zhao's nearest-feasible heuristic failed. Compare five methods: the scripted trot, the OCS2 trot, GaitNet-Full, ungated GaitNet-R and gated GaitNet-R. Report success with Wilson intervals over paired trials (which the repo's evaluation already computes), falls, edge violations and override rate. The paper's figure is success against sparsity. The gated variant should never be statistically below the trot, should rise above it where the trot fails, and its override rate should climb with sparsity.

**Weaknesses addressed.** W6 by construction. W1, because "no-op" now means "keep walking", so reluctance cannot freeze the robot. W5, because diagonal pairs are inherited and GaitNet decides when to break them, which tests the "concurrency only helps when pulled" finding directly.

**Main risk.** The gate may almost never fire, leaving a trot with extra steps. Report override rates per terrain.

### 2. Amortize gait-free contact planning and certify it asynchronously

**Idea.** Use planners that are too slow for control rate as teachers and verifiers. Teacher labels come from either of two sources. One is Zhao's gait-free MICP, whose 27 s mean solve time is acceptable for offline dataset generation ([reactive synthesis](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.23185)). The other is DIAL-MPC or MBD-style annealed full-order sampling that scores each of the 65 options (16 footholds × 4 legs plus the no-op) by short rollouts ([DIAL-MPC](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.15610v1), [MBD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.01573)). Distillation follows PDP's noisy-state/clean-label recipe, then RL fine-tuning ([PDP](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.00960)); because the teacher sees a horizon, its labels step before the body drifts. Deployment follows the Benders split ([Benders TAMP](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.13407)): GaitNet proposes its top-k at 25 Hz as the master, and a one- or two-step convex check runs asynchronously as the subproblem, vetoing infeasible proposals. The check can be an A1-style MICP with one region per step, cheapened by Logic Network Flow relaxations ([LNF](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.24235)), or simply the MPC's own QP. A CQN-AS-style critic over short contact-event sequences adds lookahead beyond one tick ([CQN-AS](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.12155)).

**Why it is novel.** Zhao's lab cannot run gait-free contact planning online: a runtime repair for an unforeseen gap stalled on **12.18 s** of MICP ([reactive synthesis](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.23185)). DIAL-MPC takes footholds as inputs, and DIAL-MPC itself lists learning a nominal policy or value as future work ([DIAL-MPC](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.15610v1)). The only learned prior in the LIDAR Lab's contact search is an RBF experience field that feeds A* under a user-defined swing order ([rebar A*](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.08354v3)). GaitNet becomes the learned, control-rate approximation of gait-free contact planning, with a certificate covering its weakest flank.

**Experiment.** Rebuild A1-ext's Unstructured and Rebar families and QuadPiPS's ten environments in Isaac Lab. Compare gait-fixed MICP, QuadPiPS-style A*, a nearest-feasible trot, RL-only GaitNet, distilled GaitNet, and distilled GaitNet with the certificate. Report success, per-decision latency, the share of certified decisions, and edge violations.

**Weaknesses addressed.** W3 (candidate quality becomes tied to downstream feasibility), W1 (proactive teacher labels) and W6 (lookahead).

**Caveat.** Scoring 65 options with DIAL-style rollouts costs roughly 65 MPC instances per label, and no source measures this. Keep it offline until benchmarked.

### 3. Prove the footstep is a controller-agnostic interface

**Idea.** Treat (leg, foothold, timing) as an interface contract and train GaitNet so its decisions transfer across executors, in layers of increasing cost. The cheapest layer randomizes the training controller's horizon, gains, swing profile and timing. The second adds Workhorse-style two-sided augmentation: GaitNet's observations are corrupted with OCS2-like tracking errors (touchdown offset, swing-timing slip, base drift), and the controller is fed jittered commands ([Workhorse](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.09117)). The third applies ASAP to the controller rather than the robot: fit a delta on the low-level command (ground reaction forces or torques) from paired Gazebo rollouts so that convex MPC plus Δ reproduces OCS2+WBC transitions, fine-tune GaitNet with Δ frozen, and deploy without it ([ASAP](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.01143v3)). The fourth identifies the controller in context with a history encoder over commanded versus achieved footholds, in the style of the dual-history controller, RMA or LocoFormer ([dual-history](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.16889), [LocoFormer](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.23745v1)). An optional fifth adds a third executor, a SceneBot-style contact-prompted learned tracker ([SceneBot](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.27581)).

**Why it is novel.** The LeCAR, Agrawal and Pathak notes each found no paper that transfers a footstep or contact-schedule policy between two different low-level controllers. ASAP and FADA close physics gaps for motion tracking, UAN closes an actuator gap ([UAN](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.10894v1)), and LocoFormer adapts joint-level policies across bodies; none changes the executor underneath a fixed decision layer.

**Experiment.** Train on the CPU convex MPC and on `gpu_mpc`, then evaluate zero-shot on OCS2+WBC in Gazebo, climbing the ablation ladder one layer at a time. Report a transfer ratio (OCS2 success divided by training-controller success), touchdown error, and the shift in no-op share. The batched GPU controller is a deliberate copy of the CPU MPC, quirks included, so it is a natural near-zero-gap control that calibrates the measurement.

**Weaknesses addressed.** W2 directly. It also turns GaitNet into the contact-decision layer that WoCoCo and SceneBot ask for.

### 4. Learn the foothold C-space at camera rate, keep the choice explicit

**Idea.** First profile and fix the 3-4 Hz pipeline. The research version is a student that maps depth plus proprioceptive history straight to GaitNet's per-leg validity grid and candidate heights at camera rate. It follows Agile Jumping's 100 Hz CNN+GRU predictor, trained DAgger-style in about an hour ([Agile Jumping](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.10923v2)), and Agarwal's GRU memory for terrain under the hind feet ([Agarwal et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2211.07638v1)). The repo's existing `distill` machinery, with a truth-reading teacher and a camera-map student, can host it. Three further pieces support it: latency randomization, as in Extreme Parkour's fixed 0.08 s and PHP's 60-80 ms ([PHP](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.15827)); conformal-calibrated validity margins that shrink the valid set when the terrain estimate is uncertain ([conformal biped](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.07725)); and Locomotion Beyond Feet's confidence gating and hysteresis.

**Why it is novel.** End-to-end students choose footholds implicitly and fail at edges. QuadPiPS keeps explicit footholds but searches with A* under a prescribed gait sequence. Explicit learned selection over a learned, calibrated validity grid is the combination neither line has, and it makes a falsifiable claim: at equal perception rate, explicit foothold selection lands fewer feet near edges than an end-to-end depth policy.

**Experiment.** Sweep perception rate (3, 10 and 30 Hz) with randomized latency across three stacks: map plus planes, the learned C-space, and an end-to-end depth policy built from Extreme Parkour's code. Report falls attributable to stale terrain, edge-violation rate and success.

**Weaknesses addressed.** W4, and W3, because validity becomes a learned, calibrated quantity.

### 5. Score contact transitions, not ticks, once the no-op math is ruled out

**Idea.** Run the free diagnosis above first. Then replace one-tick argmax with a critic over short contact-transition sequences, in which a transition is a set of at most two legs, their footholds and timing. The critic borrows CQN-AS's coarse-to-fine cells and its robustness to no-ops, FPO++-style correlated proposals across legs, HEPO constraints that keep a stepping heuristic from being traded away, and WoCoCo's stage-count reward. Condition the policy on a WTW-style behaviour vector (target cadence, maximum concurrency, pairing preference) while the policy still chooses the contacts. Analyze the resulting gaits with the hexapod paper's domain-wall metrics: switches per cycle and asymmetry ([hexapod](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.09123v1)).

**Why it is novel.** Novelty here is thinner than in Directions 1-4. The new element is behaviour conditioning over *contact-level* actions. WTW commands phase offsets and enforces them through rewards.

**Experiment.** Measure no-op share and step-initiation lag relative to the support margin, whether two concurrent swings help on terrain where the body is not being pulled forward, and cadence-tracking error under commanded behaviour vectors.

**Weaknesses addressed.** W1 and W5.

Two further-out options rank below these five. A **humanoid GaitNet** (two legs, at most one swing) on RPL-style stepping stones would be new, since no surveyed humanoid paper selects footholds explicitly. An **"omni-bodied" footstep planner**, trained over procedurally varied leg geometry with a per-robot MPC, would exploit the morphology-agnostic hip-frame action that LocoFormer's joint-space policy lacks.

## Conclusion

The survey changes what GaitNet should claim more than what it should build. The field's evidence currently favours gaits and implicit foothold choice, and our own 45% vs 18% agrees. A paper whose thesis is "non-gaited planning beats gaited" will be refuted with its own numbers. The unoccupied and defensible thesis is that **learned discrete contact decisions are the right interface between perception and any low-level executor**. Each of the top three directions tests one claim in support of that thesis: that discrete contact decisions can edit a gait without ever doing worse than it, that they can approximate an offline gait-free planner at control rate with a certificate, and that they transfer across executors. These directions also answer the two invitations the literature leaves open: WoCoCo's and SceneBot's missing contact planner, and Walk These Ways' missing automatic behaviour selector.

Two of the six weaknesses may be partly artifacts, and both are cheap to check before research effort goes into them. The no-op rate may track candidate count through the selection math, and the 3-4 Hz perception rate sits well below what a comparable pipeline reaches. A third finding reframes the evaluation itself. If the nearest-valid trot wins on today's grid voids but the same heuristic fails on sparser rebar, then the terrain, not only the policy, is hiding whatever value contact reasoning has. A sparsity sweep is the experiment that makes every direction above measurable.
